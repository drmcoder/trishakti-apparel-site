tried (`buyer-master-list.md`, `outreach-tracker.csv`, `seo-strategy.md`, `inbound-seo-playbook.md`,

restatement of those.

   Sedex is paid-but-unstarted: SAQ at 0%, no SMETA.

## 1. Diagnosis — why nothing has landed

### 1.1 The volume is ~10× too low
`outreach-tracker.csv`: **41 SENT, 28 TO SEND, zero replies logged.**

2026 cold-email benchmarks: platform-wide reply rates have fallen to **3.43%**; good campaigns
reach **5–10%**. On 41 sends, the statistically expected outcome is **1–4 replies** — and zero is
comfortably inside normal variance. **Nothing in that data says the pitch is wrong.** It says the
sample is too small to have learned anything yet.

To hold **5 live conversations**, budget roughly:

| Channel | Reply rate | Touches needed for 5 replies |
|---|---|---|
| Cold email (current) | 3–5% | **100–170** |
| Cold email, well-researched | 5–10% | 50–100 |
| LinkedIn message | 10–18% | **28–50** |
| LinkedIn InMail | 18–25% | 20–28 |

### 1.2 The list is built for a company you aren't
The **2026-07-20 fabric correction** established Trishakti works across *all* knit fabrics —
polyester and poly-blends, CVC, performance/athleisure, modal/viscose/bamboo, fleece and French
terry — trend-driven, not organic-cotton.

`buyer-master-list.md` was researched *before* that correction. People Tree, Aiayu, Dedicated,
ThokkThokk, Terra Thread, Maggie's, Ethical Tee Co. and most of the "HIGH FIT" tier are
**certified-organic-cotton brands**. They will ask for GOTS on the first reply. That list will
keep returning zero no matter how many emails go out — the misfit is structural, not a
copywriting problem.

### 1.3 The compliance gate is still shut
CBI (the Dutch government's import-promotion agency, and the most authoritative free source on
selling into the EU) lists what manufacturers must demonstrate to European buyers, in order:
**social and environmental compliance certification**, clear MOQ, realistic lead times, and a
professional website. Item one is missing.

Current state: Sedex membership **paid** (£230.61, 2026-07-13), SAQ **0%**, Environment SAQ **0%**,
SMETA **not booked**. The SAQ is free and self-service. Leaving it at 0% while paying for the
membership is the worst of both worlds.

> ⚠️ `compliance-progress.md` contradicts itself — the header says membership "NOT paid / DECIDED
> NOT TO PAY", the cost table says "✅ Paid 2026-07-13". Worth resolving; it changes what the next
> step costs.

## 2. The clock — and why it now points at the UK

**Today is 29 July 2026. Nepal graduates from LDC status on 24 November 2026 — 118 days.**

| Market | What happens | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **🇬🇧 UK** | DCTS improved its garment rules of origin so **only one significant manufacturing process** must happen in-country, and Enhanced Preference countries follow the **same** garment rules as Comprehensive ones. Nepal keeps comprehensive benefits to **2029**, then moves to Enhanced — **and the single-transformation apparel rule comes with it.** | **Primary market.** Your Chinese-fabric model survives graduation here. Nothing else in the plan matters as much as this. |
| **🇪🇺 EU** | 3-year transition secured → EBA duty-free holds to **2029**. After that, GSP+ (if Nepal even qualifies) is expected to require **double transformation** — fabric from yarn. Imported Chinese fabric would no longer confer origin. | **Sell hard now, on a deadline.** Any EU relationship must be landed and running well before 2029. |
| **🇺🇸 US** | Section 122's 10% surcharge **expired 24 July 2026** (five days ago) and was replaced the same minute by **Section 301 duties of 10–12.5% on ~80 countries**, with *no* statutory expiry. Nepal's specific placement isn't yet confirmed in published lists. Nepal never had duty-free US access anyway. | **Deprioritise.** And do not quote a US landed cost until a broker confirms Nepal's Section 301 line — the ground moved this month. |
| **🇨🇦 Canada / 🇦🇺 AU** | LDCT / duty-free; both also tied to LDC status. | Secondary, same 2026–29 urgency as the EU. |

**Also gone: the export cash subsidy.** The 4–8% export incentive is effectively dead — the
department stopped accepting applications in September 2024, and Nepal cannot continue offering
cash export subsidies after graduation. **Do not price any quote assuming it.**

### How to use the clock in a sales message
This is the best cold-open you have, and it is honest:

> "Nepal loses LDC status on 24 November. The UK has confirmed that garments cut and sewn in
> Nepal keep 0% duty under DCTS — including after graduation — because the UK requires only one
> transformation. Into the EU it holds until 2029. If you're paying 12% on knit basics today,
> there's a window worth locking in."

It has a date, a mechanism, a number and a deadline — the four things that make a sourcing manager
reply.

## 3. Channels, ranked by expected return

### TIER A — next 30 days

**A1. LinkedIn company page + founder profile — the single biggest missing asset**
It is both the highest-reply-rate outreach channel and the first place a buyer checks that you're
real. `directory-listings.md` already flags its absence as "the biggest single gap." It is free.
- Company page with the same NAP block, the factory photos, and the new team photos.
- Founder profile rewritten as *"Export manager, knit private-label manufacturing, Nepal"* — buyers
  search by role.
- Then work **sourcing managers / product developers / production managers** at target brands.
  Warm first (view profile, react to a post), message second. Never pitch in the connection request.
- Join and post in sourcing groups (e.g. "Apparel Sourcing Group"), which CBI explicitly recommends.

**A2. Rebuild the target list around what you actually make**
Four segments, replacing the organic-cotton list:

| Segment | Why they fit | What they need from you |
|---|---|---|
| **Merch / promo blanks distributors** (EU/UK) | Buy tees, polos, hoodies, tanks in 500–1,000/style. **Fabric-agnostic — poly and CVC are preferred for print.** Repeat, year-round, unbranded. | Consistency, price, on-time. No GOTS. **Least demanding entry, best fit — and nobody has worked it yet.** |
| **Streetwear / trend private label** | Poly-blends, fleece, French terry, heavyweight — exactly your GSM 135–400 range. Small brands, low MOQ. | Low MOQ, own-design/CAD, quality. |
| **Activewear / athleisure** | Poly-spandex is your stated capability and a segment most Nepal factories can't serve. | Fabric competence, fit consistency. |
| **Fabric-agnostic sourcing agents / buying houses** | One relationship routes many brands' orders. | **Social audit — this is the tier that will not move without SMETA.** |

**A3. Complete the free half of compliance this week**
Sedex SAQ + Environment SAQ, 0% → 100%. Costs nothing, is self-service, and gives you something
concrete to say instead of "audits in progress". Then book SMETA (~$2,000–4,000, 1–3 months) —
or let a serious buyer trigger a buyer-funded amfori BSCI, which is common and free to you.

**A4. Book Source Fashion, London — 19–21 January 2027, ExCeL**
The single best-fit show on the calendar:
- It sells into the **UK** — the market whose duty-free rule survives your graduation.
- 4,000+ buyers, audited manufacturers from 25+ countries, national pavilions (Sri Lanka and
  Jordan already confirmed for Jan 2027).
- **TEPC organises and subsidises Nepali participation in international fairs** — apply through
  them, don't self-fund a stand blind.
- Book now. Six months out is the right lead time; January slots and pavilion places go early.

**A5. Apparel Sourcing Paris (Texworld Evolution, Paris Le Bourget)**
CBI calls it *"the biggest garment sourcing fair in Europe."* Runs twice yearly (spring, and
late Aug/early Sep — the 2026 autumn edition was 31 Aug–2 Sep at Le Bourget). Second priority
after London because the EU window closes in 2029 and the UK's doesn't.

### TIER B — 60–90 days

**B1. Work the promotional-products channel properly**
This is the biggest unworked opportunity in your file. European promo distributors and garment
decorators buy your exact product mix, in your exact quantities, in your exact fabrics, and they
do not ask for organic certification.
- **PSI 2027 — 12–14 January 2027.** Note: **it has moved from Düsseldorf to Cologne.** 600+
  exhibitors, 11,000+ visitors from 70 countries; Europe's leading promotional-products fair.
  It falls the week *before* Source Fashion London — **one trip, two shows.**
- Between now and then, target promo distributors and screen printers/decorators directly.
  Their pain is exactly what you solve: low MOQ, duty-free, and someone who answers emails.

**B2. Reconsider Foursource**
`directory-listings.md` says "skip — paid subscription." Worth revisiting: it's Berlin-based,
apparel/textile-specific, and structured around **buyers posting RFQs** rather than suppliers
shouting into a directory. It is the one platform where EU buyers actively request quotes in your
exact category, with verified certificates and escrowed orders. Get a current supplier quote and
judge it on cost-per-conversation, not on backlinks.
*(The Alibaba verdict stands — wrong buyer, wrong economics, $1,999+/yr.)*

**B3. Sourcing agents and buying houses**
One relationship can carry many brands' orders. This tier needs the audit first, so it sequences
naturally after A3. Note the local precedent: **Logo Industries** built a Europe-facing Nepal
business and won a Nepal-German Chamber best-exporter award — bilateral chamber membership is a
proven, cheap trust signal here, and both competitors who outrank you use it.

### TIER C — keep running, don't over-invest
Diaspora stores (97 rows already built), India via the Treaty of Trade 40%-value-addition route.
Low ticket, but they produce **real export references and shipping history** — which is what
makes the Tier A buyers take you seriously.

## 4. SEO — what changed, and the one move to make today

### 4.1 Buy the .com. It's available and it's $11.25.
`seo-strategy.md` §0.5 calls this "the highest-leverage single move" — Google reads `.com.np` as
*"targets Nepal"*, and a ccTLD's geo-target **cannot be overridden** in Search Console. Every buyer
you want is abroad.

**Checked today:**

| Domain | Status |
|---|---|
| **trishaktiapparel.com** | ✅ **Available — $11.25/yr** |
| trishaktiknit.com | ✅ Available — $11.25/yr |
| trishakti.com | ❌ Taken |
| trishaktiapparel.co.uk | ✅ Available |

**Recommendation:** buy `trishaktiapparel.com` **today**, defensively, whatever you decide about
migrating. At $11.25 the downside is zero and the downside of someone else taking your name is not.

**On migrating:** do it, and do it *now* rather than later — a migration costs you a few months of
ranking turbulence, and you currently have little ranking to lose. That is the cheapest this move
will ever be. Keep `.com.np` registered and 301 it to the `.com`; keep it on the letterhead for
Nepali institutional credibility.

### 4.2 AI search is now a real acquisition channel
Gartner projects traditional search volume down **25% by 2026** as buyers shift to conversational
AI. Younger B2B buyers now shortlist suppliers in ChatGPT/Perplexity/Copilot before they ever open
Google. If a buyer asks *"low-MOQ knit manufacturer with duty-free EU access"* and you aren't named,
you lose a lead you never see.

You're **already ahead** here — `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt` are live, which almost no Nepal peer
has. What's missing is the *corroboration* AI engines look for. In priority order, all free:

| Do this | Why |
|---|---|
| **LinkedIn company page** | Highest-authority corroborating source; read by every AI engine. Also A1 above. |
| **Wikidata item** | Feeds Google's Knowledge Graph directly. Purest AEO play available. |
| **Bing Places** | Feeds Bing → which powers **ChatGPT Search and Copilot**. Almost nobody bothers. |
| **Crunchbase** | Frequently cited by answer engines. |
| **DUNS number** (free) | Not SEO — large buyers *require* it for vendor onboarding. |

The mechanism: AI engines cite what several independent sources agree on. One website saying you
exist isn't enough; five corroborating profiles is.

### 4.2b What Search Console actually shows (24h snapshot, 29 July 2026)
⚠️ **24 hours and ~44 impressions is a noise-level sample — pull the 3-month view before deciding
anything on it.** But the *shape* is consistent and worth recording.

| | Reading |
|---|---|
| **~44 impressions, 0 clicks** | 0% CTR means average position is deep — page 2–4. Being shown, never chosen. |
| **Countries: Nepal 18, US 6, India 4, Saudi 4, Bangladesh 4, UK 3, Philippines 2, Portugal 1** | **~75% of visibility is Nepal + South Asia + Gulf.** Germany, France, Netherlands, Denmark, Sweden, Canada, Australia — the entire target buyer geography — are **absent**. India/Bangladesh traffic is competitors; Gulf traffic is diaspora. |
| **Top pages: 8 of the top 10 are blog posts** (`/` 16, gsm-guide 9, private-label-vs-white-label 8, qc-aql 4) | The blog carries the site. `/products/*`, `/capabilities`, `/why-nepal` — the pages that sell — get essentially nothing. |
| **Queries: "how to check gsm of t shirt", "how much does a polo shirt weigh", "difference between lc and tt", "white label oem"** | **All informational. Zero commercial intent, zero geo-qualified.** No "t-shirt manufacturer Nepal", no duty-free queries. |

**The one encouraging signal:** *"difference between lc and tt"* and *"how to check gsm"* are
**first-time-importer** questions. That is genuinely your buyer — just 12–18 months before they
place an order. The content is aimed correctly; the audience is early.

### 4.2c Correction: the .com is a ceiling, not the current bottleneck
Earlier in this document (and in `seo-strategy.md`) the ccTLD is framed as "the highest-leverage
single move." The GSC data refines that:

**You are not at 0 clicks because of the domain. You are at 0 clicks because nothing links to you.**
There are **28** well-written posts on this site and near-zero referring domains, so Google shows
them at position 20–40 — a `.com` with the same (absent) backlink profile would rank identically.

So: **buy `trishaktiapparel.com` anyway** — $11.25, defensive, and correct long-term, because the
ccTLD *does* cap the ceiling once you have authority (41% of visibility landing in Nepal is that
cap showing). But do not expect the migration to fix this, and **do not let it displace link-building**,
which is the actual binding constraint. Phase 1 of `seo-strategy.md` §5 (GAN, TEPC, FNCCI, Kompass,
LinkedIn, Wikidata, Crunchbase, Bing Places) is the work that moves the number.

> **And the bigger point: SEO will not get you your first export customer.** 44 impressions/day,
> 3–6 months to rank, top-of-funnel queries. Search Console is the wrong scoreboard for a company
> with zero export customers — it is a lagging indicator of work you have not done yet. Watch
> **touches / replies / samples / quotes** (§5). Let SEO compound quietly behind the outreach.

### 4.3 Content — three pages that don't exist yet and should
Your keyword research stands; the gap is pages. Highest value first:

1. **`/uk-duty-free-sourcing`** — *"0% duty into the UK under DCTS — and it survives Nepal's
   graduation."* This targets your most durable market with your most defensible fact. It does not
   exist. Build it first.
2. **`/duty-free-sourcing`** (pillar) — the EBA / single-transformation / 24-Nov-2026 explainer.
   `seo-strategy.md` marks this "highest strategic value" and the content already sits in
   `lib/content.js`.
3. **"What changes for buyers on 24 November 2026"** — timely, uncontested, genuinely useful, and
   it doubles as your outreach subject line. Keep a visible "last verified" date on it.

The tariff facts in §2 above are fresh as of today and are exactly the substance those pages need.

### 4.3b "Should we write more blog posts?" — mostly no
You have **28 posts**, and they cover the generic first-time-importer journey thoroughly: tech
packs, GSM, Incoterms, LC vs TT, AQL, lead times, sample-to-bulk, duty, rules of origin, China+1.
There is very little left to say to that reader.

**Post #29 will land at position 20–40 exactly like the other 28.** Volume is not the constraint —
referring domains are. Writing more is the most satisfying way to avoid the actual work.

Two exceptions, both worth doing:

**(a) Refresh, don't add.** `nepal-ldc-graduation-2026-what-importers-must-know` is now **118 days**
from the date it's about, and §2 above has facts it cannot contain (EU 3-year transition confirmed
to 2029; UK DCTS Enhanced Preferences keeping single transformation; Section 122's expiry on
24 July 2026 and the Section 301 replacement; the export subsidy ending). Rewrite it with a visible
**"last verified 29 July 2026"**. Google rewards freshness on time-bound queries, it is your most
differentiated topic, and it doubles as outreach collateral. Same for
`nepal-duty-free-apparel-eu-uk-canada`.

**(b) Four posts for the four buyer segments — written as sales collateral, not as SEO.**
Every one of the 28 posts speaks to a generic small brand. **Nothing** on the site speaks to the
segments §3 says to target — which means the outreach in weeks 5–8 has nothing to link to:

| Missing post | Written for | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|
| *"Sourcing blank tees and hoodies for decoration: what a printer should ask a factory"* | **Promo/merch distributors** | The best-fit, wholly unworked segment. Zero coverage today. |
| *"Heavyweight fleece and French terry: manufacturing 300–400 GSM hoodies"* | **Streetwear** | Your GSM 135–400 range is a real differentiator and is nowhere on the blog. |
| *"Poly-spandex activewear: what to spec before you ask for a quote"* | **Activewear** | A stated capability with no content behind it at all. |
| *"0% duty into the UK under DCTS — and why it survives Nepal's graduation"* | **UK buyers** | Your most durable fact, currently buried inside a combined EU/UK/CA post. Pair it with the `/uk-duty-free-sourcing` page in §4.3. |

Judge these on **"can I paste this link into a LinkedIn message to a promo distributor?"**, not on
search volume. That payback arrives in weeks; ranking takes months.

**Cadence:** four posts + two refreshes over ~6 weeks, then stop and put the writing time into
backlinks and outreach until the referring-domain count is double digits.
